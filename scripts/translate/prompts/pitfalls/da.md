# Known pitfalls: Danish (da)

- **Compounds in one word.** "projektkalender", "standardværdi", "opgavetabel"; never split
  ("projekt kalender"). After an abbreviation or a code use a hyphen: "IFC-fil", "P6-projekt".
- **No double definiteness.** With an adjective Danish uses den/det and no suffix: "den kritiske
  vej", "det nye projekt", "de efterfølgende opgaver"; not "den kritiske vejen" (Norwegian, Swedish).
- **en or et.** "en opgave", "en afhængighed", "en tidsplan", "et projekt"; adjectives agree: "et nyt
  projekt", "nye opgaver", "opgaven er kritisk".
- **ikke in a subordinate clause.** ikke stands before the verb: "hvis opgaven ikke har slæk", not
  "hvis opgaven har ikke slæk". A main clause keeps the verb second: "Så flytter appen opgaven".
- **Danish, not Norwegian or Swedish.** "uge" (not uke), "efter" (not etter), "nu" (not nå),
  "opgave" (not oppgave), "gennem" (not gjennom), "hvordan", "også".
- **Possessive before the noun.** "din tidsplan", "dit projekt", "dine opgaver"; not "tidsplanen
  din" (Norwegian).
- **Dates and numbers.** "mandag den 7. juni 2027": lower case, "den" before the date. Decimal comma
  (0,5 dage); WBS codes such as 1.2 keep the period. One pair of quotation marks in the whole
  article.
- **False friends.** "aktuel" = current, not actual (→ "faktisk"); "eventuel" = possible.
- **Word senses.** A late task "bliver en dag forsinket" or "bliver færdig en dag senere"; the noun
  forsinkelse is only the lag on a relation. A house extension is tilbygning, not udvidelse. The break
  in the working day is pause (`terms`); the task split is afbrydelse. A mode is "tilstand" everywhere,
  not "metode" or "modus".
- **Calques.** "går tilbage til" (falls back to) → "sættes til"; "kører sent" (runs late) → "bliver
  forsinket"; "på plads" is fine for "in place".
